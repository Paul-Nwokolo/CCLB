# Photos to take next: a shot list for the parish office

The new website has places ("slots") for a few photos and a gallery of up to 12. Today the parish
has good photos of the altar and the building, but almost every photo of people needs permission
first. This list is what to take next, slot by slot, so the site shows the real parish.

## Before anyone takes a photo

1. **Announce it.** At the Mass where photos will be taken, say so from the ambo and say where
   people can sit if they would rather not appear.
2. **Permission in writing** from every adult who can be recognised in a photo used on the website.
   A one-page form is enough: name, the photo, "may be used on the parish website and social
   media", signature, date. Keep the forms in the office.
3. **Children:** never recognisable on the website without written consent from a parent or
   guardian. Prefer hands, backs and wide shots where no child's face can be made out.
4. **Remove on request**, quickly, no questions.
5. **Ask the photographer for clean files** (no watermark) and how they want to be credited.
   The site prints the credit under the photo.

## How to shoot (any good phone is fine)

- Highest resolution the camera allows; at least **3000 px on the long side**. No filters, no
  stickers, no text on the photo.
- Keep vertical lines straight (walls, pillars, the crucifix). Hold the phone level.
- Morning light from the side is best. Turn the flash off inside the church.
- For each photo, write one line saying what it shows ("The 6:30 AM Mass on a Wednesday, seen
  from the back"). That line becomes the alt text and the caption.
- Name the files plainly: `hero-baptism-water-2026-12.jpg`, not `IMG_4471.jpg`.

## Which photos already on the website can be used

See the library audit in `MEDIA.md` (section 8). In short: the altar photo and the building photos
can be used now; photos with people need permission first; three photos show children and need a
guardian's written consent; the stock photos (an empty church from Pixabay, a cross on a hill, a
statue) must never be shown as if they were this parish.
